How to Play

The goal

Checkmate the enemy king: attack it so it has no safe square, no piece can block, and nothing can capture the attacker. The king is never actually taken.

The board

Eight files (a–h) run left to right and eight ranks (1–8) run front to back. Each player has a light square in the right-hand corner. White always moves first, and players then take turns, one move each.

Special moves

  • Castling. The king moves two squares toward a rook and that rook jumps to the square the king crossed. Neither piece may have moved, the squares between them must be empty, and the king cannot be in check, pass through an attacked square, or land in check.
  • En passant. When a pawn jumps two squares and lands beside an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square. This must be done on the very next move or the chance is gone.
  • Promotion. A pawn that reaches the far rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight of its owner's choice. Most players pick a queen.

Check, mate and draws

  • Check means the king is attacked. You must escape it at once: move the king, block, or capture the attacker. You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check.
  • Checkmate is check with no escape. The game ends and the attacker wins.
  • Stalemate is when the player to move is not in check but has no legal move. The game is drawn.
  • The game is also drawn by threefold repetition of the same position, by the fifty-move rule (fifty moves each with no capture or pawn move), or when neither side has enough material to mate, such as king against king.

Controls

  • Tap one of your pieces. Gold dots mark its legal moves and gold rings mark captures. Tap a dot or ring to move.
  • Drag anywhere to rotate the board. Pinch or scroll to zoom.
  • Turn swings the camera to the other side. Undo takes back your last move. Hint lights up a strong move. Moves shows the game in standard notation.

Game modes

  • Play the Computer. Novice is forgiving, Club plays solid tactics, Master thinks for up to two seconds a move.
  • Timed games. Against the Computer, pick Bullet, Blitz or Rapid. Each side gets a clock (minutes + seconds added per move). Run out of time and you lose. Undo is off in timed games.
  • Play Online. Face another player on their own phone. One player creates a match and shares the 4-character code, the other joins with it. The host picks colors, and colors swap on every rematch. If a player stays disconnected for 45 seconds, the other wins.
  • Tutorial. Ten short lessons. Each sets up a position and asks you to find one move.
